Mechanisms & fidelity

CH4/PWM0 and CH5/PWM1: right and left eye vertical axes. CH6/PWM2 and CH7/PWM3: left and right eye horizontal axes. CH8/PWM4: linked eyelids. CH1/PWM5: neck flexion/extension; CH2/PWM6: neck rotation; CH3/PWM7: neck lateral flexion. M1 controls mouth opening. The left and right neck-bolt LEDs are represented as one shared auxiliary signal.

This is a virtual actuator test bench: percentages are normalized motion/signal estimates, never direct PWM commands. The PCA outputs are shown so browser behavior can be compared with the eventual servo and LED signal routes. The local hardware fit uses iPhone-observed directions and an approximate travel curve. The earlier presentation enlarges neck angles for visibility. Neither mode supplies measured servo feedback or verified mechanical limits.

The existing conversational emotion rules provide the same direction to ElevenLabs and the eyes and neck posture. A separate semantic cue—think, agree, disagree, listening, surprise, startle, suspicion, repair, plead, relief or withdraw—adds one intentional gesture without turning every word into a motor command. This is not acoustic emotion recognition. Reference playback uses a curious direction.

Emotion motion is semantic body language: curiosity acquires with the eyes first; attentive listening becomes still and direct; hope opens the lids and leans toward Christopher; engagement holds contact with a slow scan; suspicion leads with a coordinated side-eye and a small head turn; startle opens the gaze and briefly recoils; vulnerability looks upward and asks him to remain; relief releases tension; wariness turns aside; hurt lowers and averts; earned anger advances with a firmer stare; withdrawal looks away and becomes still. With More expressive emotion enabled (the default), these readable changes and social gestures are deliberately easier to see; turn it off for the restrained comparison. Speech adds sparse phrase-level emphasis rather than syllable-by-syllable servo chatter. The normalized LED_BOLTS value drives two dim visual proxy lights only; it is not a calibrated PWM command.
